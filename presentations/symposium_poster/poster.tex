\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{block}{Why Might Blood Vessels Predict Treatment Response?}

    \textbf{Neoadjuvant treatment (NAT)} is given before breast cancer surgery.
    \textbf{Pathologic complete response (pCR)}---no residual invasive cancer at
    surgery---is associated with better outcomes \citep{cortazar2014pcr}. Earlier
    prediction could support treatment tailoring: less intensive therapy for
    patients likely to respond well and alternative therapy for those unlikely
    to achieve pCR.

    Tumors recruit abnormal vessels that can be dense, tortuous, leaky, and 
    disorganized \citep{nagy2009tumourvessels}. Their structure and
    contrast-enhancement dynamics may reflect tumor biology and affect drug 
    delivery. We test whether these vessel-network features improve pCR 
    prediction beyond simple summaries of the same MRI 
    measurements.
  \end{block}

    \begin{highlightbox}[block example body]
      \textbf{Research Question:} does the structure of breast blood
      vessel networks and the way contrast changes through them improve response
      prediction beyond simple summaries of the same MRI measurements?
    \end{highlightbox}

  \begin{block}{UChicago Cohort: 179 Labeled, 1,226 Unlabeled Exams}

    UChicago contributed \textbf{181 labeled exams from 143 patients};
    \textbf{179 exams from 141 patients} are analyzed here; \textbf{48 of the 141
    patients (34\%)} achieved pCR. Two exams lacked the required centerline,
    support mask, and aligned MRI.

    Acquisition timing is heterogeneous: exams contain 13--24 frames spanning
    96--480\,s. Longitudinal exams are included. This analysis measures pCR
    discrimination across longitudinal exams; baseline-only early prediction
    remains to be tested. Cross-validation is grouped by patient, so one
    patient's exams never cross training/evaluation splits.

    Pretraining draws on a separate, non-overlapping pool: \textbf{1,226
    unlabeled exams from 225 patients}, with no
    patient or exam shared with the labeled cohort.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Three Graph Representations Test Whether Topology Matters}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/graph_representation.pdf}
      \caption{The same vessel centerline represented three ways.}
    \end{figure}

    We represent the vascular network of each patient's breasts as a
    graph:

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Voxel graph:} each vessel-centerline voxel is a node;
      neighboring centerline voxels are connected.
      \item \textbf{Junction graph:} branch points are nodes
      ($v\in V_{\mathrm{vox}}$, $\deg(v)>2$); vessel segments connect them.
      \item \textbf{Segment graph:} vessel segments are nodes; segments sharing
      an endpoint are connected.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{block}

\end{column}

\separatorcolumn

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{block}{Vessel Centerlines Become Graphs for pCR Prediction}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{figures/method_pipeline.pdf}
      \caption{Ultrafast breast MRI movie to vessel-network prediction.}
    \end{figure}

    A published deep-learning method (nnU-Net) identifies vessel voxels
    \citep{isensee2021nnunet}. Our \textbf{tc4d} algorithm (temporal-consensus
    4D, using consistency across DCE-MRI time points to extract an exam-level
    vessel skeleton) converts that mask into a one-voxel-wide centerline while
    preserving vascular connectivity. The centerline then becomes one of the
    three graph representations.

    Nodes carry geometry and measured enhancement curves. A \textbf{graph neural
    network (GNN)} combines neighboring-node information, pools the network into
    one exam representation, and predicts pCR.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Forecasting Pretraining: A Controlled 2x2 Comparison}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/arch4.pdf}
      \caption{Forecasting weights transfer into the pCR classifier; a randomly
      initialized twin is the control arm.}
    \end{figure}

    The encoder receives vessel curves, connections, and elapsed times and
    predicts \textbf{future enhancement at connected vessel nodes}
    \citep{tang2022selfsupervised}. The downstream comparison holds inputs,
    encoder, training, patient-grouped folds, and evaluation fixed:
    forecasting versus random initialization, with and without graph message
    passing.

    Centerline enhancement features predict pCR modestly, but hand-engineered
    GNNs do not beat matched tabular summaries---motivating this pretraining
    comparison as an alternative route to a better node representation.

  \end{block}

\end{column}

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{alertblock}{Forecast Pretraining Improves the GNN}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.72\linewidth]{figures/results_auc.pdf}
      \caption{Patient-level pooled out-of-fold AUC (each patient's exam
      probabilities averaged to one prediction) with patient-bootstrap 95\%
      CIs on shared patient-grouped folds.}
    \end{figure}

    The GNN uses voxel graphs (one node per centerline voxel), matching the
    existing pipeline rather than swept against segment/junction
    representations.

    Paired pretraining $\Delta$AUC: \textbf{+0.149} [\textbf{+0.046},
    \textbf{+0.255}] for the GNN, versus +0.034 [$-$0.049, +0.120] for
    graph-free (95\% CI crosses zero). The pretrained GNN exceeded pretrained
    graph-free by $\Delta$AUC +0.079 [+0.010, +0.155].

    % The claim is narrow: initialization helped this GNN on one seed, and its
    % absolute interval includes chance.

  \end{alertblock}

  \begin{block}{Prediction Is Sensitive to Acquisition Timing}

    Acquisition timing is associated with outcome in this cohort: a model using
    frame count, duration, and cadence without imaging features reaches
    \textbf{0.587} AUC---essentially the pretrained GNN's 0.583. Their predictions
    correlate (Pearson $r=0.50$).

    This raises concern about protocol dependence, but cannot separate technical
    from biological association. Adjustment can remove genuine patient
    differences that happen to track acquisition as well as technical variation.

    \begin{highlightbox}
      Forecast pretraining improved the vessel GNN, which outperformed the
      matched graph-free model. Acquisition timing was also associated with
      outcome, motivating follow-up analyses to distinguish protocol-related
      and vascular contributions to prediction.
    \end{highlightbox}

  \end{block}
