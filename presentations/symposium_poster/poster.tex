\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{block}{Why Might Blood Vessels Predict Treatment Response?}

    \textbf{Neoadjuvant treatment (NAT)} varies by breast cancer subtype and may
    include chemotherapy and HER2-targeted therapy. Only some patients achieve
    a \textbf{pathologic complete response (pCR)}, which is associated with
    improved long-term outcomes \citep{cortazar2014pcr}. Earlier prediction could
    avoid ineffective treatment for women unlikely to benefit and identify
    nonresponders who need intensified or alternative therapy.

    Tumors recruit abnormal vessels that can be dense, tortuous, leaky, and 
    disorganized \citep{nagy2009tumourvessels}. Their structure and
    contrast-enhancement dynamics may reflect tumor biology and affect drug 
    delivery. We test whether these vessel-network features improve pCR 
    prediction beyond simple summaries of the same MRI 
    measurements.
  \end{block}

    \begin{highlightbox}
      \textbf{Research Question:} does the structure of breast blood
      vessel networks and the way contrast changes through them improve response
      prediction beyond simple summaries of the same MRI measurements?
    \end{highlightbox}

  \begin{block}{UChicago Cohort: 179 Labeled, 1,226 Unlabeled Exams}

    UChicago contributed \textbf{181 labeled exams from 143 patients};
    \textbf{179 exams from 141 patients} are analyzed here (115 non-pCR /
    \textbf{64 pCR}, 36\%). Two exams lacked the required centerline, support
    mask, and aligned MRI.

    Acquisition timing is heterogeneous: exams contain 13--24 frames spanning
    96--480\,s. Longitudinal exams are included. Cross-validation is grouped by
    patient, so one patient's exams never cross training/evaluation splits.

    Pretraining draws on a separate, non-overlapping pool: \textbf{1,226
    unlabeled exams from 225 patients} (1,081 CAPS, 145 CHIMEC), with no
    patient or exam shared with the labeled cohort.

  \end{block}

  \begin{alertblock}{Three Graph Representations Test Whether Topology Matters}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.66\linewidth]{figures/graph_representation.pdf}
      \caption{The same vessel centerline represented three ways.}
    \end{figure}

    We represent the vascular network of each patient's breasts as a
    graph:

    \begin{itemize}
      \item \textbf{Voxel graph:} each vessel-centerline voxel is a node;
      neighboring centerline voxels are connected.
      \item \textbf{Junction graph:} branch points are nodes
      ($v\in V_{\mathrm{vox}}$, $\deg(v)>2$); vessel segments connect them.
      \item \textbf{Segment graph:} vessel segments are nodes; segments sharing
      an endpoint are connected.
    \end{itemize}

  \end{alertblock}

\end{column}

\separatorcolumn

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{block}{Vessel Centerlines Become Graphs for pCR Prediction}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{figures/method_pipeline.pdf}
      \caption{Ultrafast breast MRI movie to vessel-network prediction.}
    \end{figure}

    A published deep-learning method (nnU-Net) identifies vessel voxels
    \citep{isensee2021nnunet}. Our \textbf{tc4d} algorithm converts that mask into
    a one-voxel-wide centerline while preserving vascular connectivity. The
    centerline then becomes one of the three graph representations.

    Nodes carry geometry and measured enhancement curves. A \textbf{graph neural
    network (GNN)} combines neighboring-node information, pools the network into
    one exam representation, and predicts pCR.

  \end{block}

  \begin{block}{Forecasting Pretraining Helps This GNN; Clinical Value Remains Unproven}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.58\linewidth]{figures/architecture.pdf}
      \caption{Forecasting weights transfer into the pCR classifier; a randomly
      initialized twin is the control arm.}
    \end{figure}

    The encoder receives vessel curves, connections, and elapsed times and
    predicts \textbf{future enhancement at connected vessel nodes}
    \citep{tang2022selfsupervised}. The downstream comparison holds inputs,
    encoder, training, patient-grouped folds, and evaluation fixed:
    forecasting versus random initialization, with and without graph message
    passing.

    Centerline enhancement features predict pCR modestly, but hand-engineered
    GNNs do not beat matched tabular summaries. Forecasting pretraining improved
    this downstream GNN; graph-free pretraining was unresolved. Repeated seeds
    and locked-cohort validation must reproduce the gain before any clinical or
    vascular-specific claim.

  \end{block}

\end{column}

\separatorcolumn

\begin{column}{\colwidth}

  \begin{exampleblock}{Forecast Pretraining Improves the GNN in One Development Run}

    \begin{figure}
      \centering
      \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{figures/results_auc.pdf}
      \caption{Patient-level pooled out-of-fold AUC with patient-bootstrap 95\%
      CIs on shared patient-grouped folds.}
    \end{figure}

    Forecasting initialization changed GNN AUC from 0.434 to \textbf{0.583}:
    paired $\Delta$AUC \textbf{+0.149} [\textbf{+0.046}, \textbf{+0.255}]. The
    graph-free effect was unresolved: +0.034 [$-$0.049, +0.120]. The pretrained
    GNN exceeded pretrained graph-free by +0.079 [+0.010, +0.155].

    The claim is narrow: initialization helped this GNN on one seed. Its absolute
    interval includes chance. Earlier feature-matched experiments found that full
    kinetics drove most performance, with no measurable message-passing benefit.

  \end{exampleblock}

  \begin{block}{Prediction Is Sensitive to Acquisition Timing}

    Acquisition timing is associated with outcome in this cohort: a model using
    frame count, duration, and cadence without imaging features reaches
    \textbf{0.587} AUC---essentially the pretrained GNN's 0.583. Their predictions
    correlate (Pearson $r=0.50$).

    This raises concern about protocol dependence, but cannot separate technical
    from biological association. Adjustment can remove genuine patient
    differences that happen to track acquisition as well as technical variation.

  \end{block}
